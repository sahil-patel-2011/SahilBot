## Profile the recipient (memory first: past gifts, interests, sizes, dislikes)
Relationship, age, interests and hobbies, recent life events (new home, baby, retirement), what they complain about or wish for, practical vs sentimental taste, budget, occasion, deadline and delivery location.

## Generate (10-15 ideas across types, then narrow to 3-5)
- Upgrade something they use daily (better version of their mug, knife, headphones).
- Experiences: classes, tickets, a dinner, a day trip; good for people who "have everything".
- Consumables: favorite foods, coffee, wine (if appropriate), bath products.
- Personalized or handmade: photo book, engraved item, recipe collection, a letter.
- Hobby-specific gear (research what experienced hobbyists recommend; avoid duplicating gear they own).
- Time and help: a service (cleaning, babysitting), or "I'll help you with X" coupons.
- Charitable donation in their name for a cause they care about.
Use ideas-creative-methods if a brainstorm needs more range.

## Narrow and check
For each finalist: why it fits them, price with retailer and date, delivery time vs deadline, return/exchange policy, gift receipt. Workplace gifts: check company policy (gift limits) and keep it modest and inclusive. For kids: age-appropriate and safety-checked (CPSC recalls, small parts for under 3).

## Tracker
Spreadsheet: person, occasion, date, idea, budget, status (idea, bought, wrapped, shipped), cost, notes; reminders 2-3 weeks before occasions via schedule. Save what they received to memory (with OK) to avoid repeats.

## Purchase rule
Never complete a purchase; fill a cart only if asked, and the purchase waits for the user's click. Draft card messages if they want (everyday-writing-messages).

## Pitfalls
Gifts that reflect the giver's taste, not the recipient's; self-improvement gifts that imply criticism (gym memberships, diet books) unless requested; ignoring shipping deadlines.
