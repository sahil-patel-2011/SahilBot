## Chores
1. List all recurring tasks with frequency and minutes (daily dishes 20, weekly bathrooms 45, monthly fridge clean-out 20...).
2. Divide by time and preference, not just count; rotate disliked tasks; age-appropriate tasks for kids (toddlers put toys away, school-age set the table, teens laundry and cooking).
3. Make it visible: a shared chart (spreadsheet or printable PDF via create_document) or shared reminders lists (mac_apps Reminders, Todoist connector).
4. Weekly 10-minute check-in to rebalance.

## Cleaning schedule (simple rhythm)
Daily: dishes, wipe counters, 10-minute tidy. Weekly: bathrooms, floors, sheets, trash and recycling, fridge check. Monthly: appliances, baseboards, dust high areas, clean out a drawer. Seasonal: windows, deep clean, closets. Never mix bleach with ammonia or acids (toxic gases); ventilate.

## Paperwork and bills
One inbox tray or folder; process weekly; scan and file digitally (by year and category); bills calendar with autopay status; shred documents with personal data. Retention: check IRS guidance for tax records.

## Important documents binder (physical + encrypted digital copy)
IDs and passports (copies), birth and marriage certificates, insurance policies, wills and powers of attorney, medical info and medicine lists, account list (no passwords in the binder; use a password manager), home and vehicle titles, emergency contacts. Tell a trusted person where it is.

## Home inventory
Room-by-room video or photos plus a spreadsheet of valuable items (description, date, price, serial number, receipt). Store a copy off-site or in the cloud; update yearly. Helps insurance claims.

## Family calendar
Shared calendar (Apple Calendar via mac_apps, Google Calendar connector) with color per person; Sunday 15-minute planning meeting for the week; recurring events for trash day, activities, bills.

## Tools
Use memory for household facts (trash day, filter size) with the user's OK; schedule for recurring nudges. Never send messages or invites to others without the user's approval.

## Pitfalls
Systems too complex to keep up; one person carrying invisible work (planning, remembering); no backup copies of documents.
