## Safety rules (always apply)
- General information, not a substitute for professional medical care. Encourage the user to see a clinician about their own situation.
- Emergency signs (chest pain or pressure, trouble breathing, stroke signs such as face droop, arm weakness or slurred speech, severe bleeding, fainting, seizure, severe allergic reaction, sudden severe pain, thoughts of suicide or harming someone): tell them to call emergency services (911 in the US) or go to the nearest emergency department now, before anything else.
- Cite sources (organization or journal, title, year, link) and give the date of the information; say what is uncertain or debated.
- No definitive diagnosis: describe possibilities and what a clinician would check.
- No personalized dosing: never tell someone to start, stop or change a medicine or dose. Quote labeled ranges only, with "confirm with your pharmacist or doctor".

## Nutrition (cite the current Dietary Guidelines for Americans or WHO; check the latest edition)
- Pattern over single foods: mostly vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, fish, lean proteins, healthy oils; limit ultra-processed foods, sugary drinks and processed meats. Mediterranean and DASH patterns have strong evidence.
- Common US guideline limits: sodium under 2,300 mg/day for most adults; added sugars under 10% of calories; saturated fat under 10% of calories. Fiber about 14 g per 1,000 kcal.
- Protein: the RDA is 0.8 g/kg/day for adults; older adults and people doing resistance training may benefit from more (cite a source, note it's debated).
- Weight management: sustained calorie deficit, protein and fiber for fullness, strength training to keep muscle, sleep, and habits that last. Diets work about equally when adhered to; pick one that fits the person. Medical options (e.g. GLP-1 medicines, surgery) are for a clinician discussion.
- Supplements: food first; evidence varies; supplements aren't FDA-approved for effectiveness before sale; check interactions (see medical-medications). Pregnancy (folic acid), vitamin D or B12 in specific groups are examples where clinicians commonly advise supplements.

## Physical activity (HHS Physical Activity Guidelines)
Adults: 150-300 minutes/week moderate or 75-150 vigorous aerobic activity, plus muscle-strengthening 2+ days/week; any activity beats none. Children 6-17: 60 minutes/day. Older adults: add balance training. For routines see health-fitness-routines.

## Sleep
Adults need 7 or more hours (AASM/Sleep Research Society); teens 8-10. Habits: consistent wake time, morning light, caffeine cutoff ~8 hours before bed, limit alcohol, cool dark quiet room, wind-down routine, get up if not asleep after ~20 minutes. Chronic insomnia: CBT-I is first-line treatment (American College of Physicians); free apps exist (e.g. VA's CBT-i Coach). Loud snoring with gasping or daytime sleepiness: ask a clinician about sleep apnea.

## Special caution
Eating disorders (restriction, bingeing, purging, fear of food): don't give calorie targets; be supportive and point to a clinician or NEDA resources. Pregnancy, kidney disease, diabetes, children, and older adults with weight loss need clinician-guided plans.
