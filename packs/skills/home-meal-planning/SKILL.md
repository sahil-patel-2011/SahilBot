## Gather (memory first)
Household size and ages, allergies and diets (allergy safety first), dislikes, cooking time per night, equipment (slow cooker, air fryer), budget per week, store(s), what's already in the pantry/fridge, nights eating out.

## Plan
1. Pick 5-6 dinners (leave 1-2 nights for leftovers or flexible meals) balancing protein sources, a vegetable at each meal, and one new recipe at most.
2. Reuse ingredients across meals (a roast chicken → tacos → soup) to cut waste and cost.
3. Prep plan: one 60-90 minute session (wash/chop vegetables, cook grains, marinate, make a sauce).
4. Breakfasts and lunches as simple repeatable templates.
5. Nutrition: follow the household's goals using health-nutrition-sleep-exercise basics; for medical diets (diabetes, kidney disease, low sodium) keep to the care team's guidance.

## Grocery list
Combined quantities, grouped by store section (produce, meat/fish, dairy, pantry, frozen, household), with checkboxes; mark items already on hand. Estimate cost from the store's site when easy (cite the date) or typical prices; show the total vs budget. Output as a document, a note (mac_apps Notes or Reminders list), or a spreadsheet.

## Saving money
Plan around sales and seasonal produce, store brands, cook beans and grains in bulk, freeze portions, use what's on hand first, buy whole and portion it.

## Food safety basics
Fridge at or below 40°F (4°C), freezer 0°F (-18°C); leftovers within 2 hours into the fridge and eaten in about 3-4 days (USDA). Thaw in the fridge, cold water or microwave, not on the counter.

## Rules
Online grocery orders: fill the cart only if the user asks; the purchase always waits for their click. Save favorite meals and diet facts to memory with their OK.

## Pitfalls
Too many new recipes, ignoring busy nights, buying perishables for meals late in the week, forgetting staples (oil, salt, spices).
