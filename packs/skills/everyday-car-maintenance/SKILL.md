## Start from the owner's manual
Find the model's maintenance schedule (manual or the manufacturer's site). Modern cars often go 5,000-10,000+ miles between oil changes depending on oil type and driving conditions ("severe service" for short trips, towing, dust, extreme heat): follow the manual, not the old 3,000-mile rule.

## Regular checks
- Monthly: tire pressure (cold, using the placard on the driver's door jamb, not the tire sidewall max), tread depth (2/32 inch is the legal minimum in most US states; the penny test: if you see all of Lincoln's head, replace; many experts suggest replacing near 4/32 for wet roads), lights, wipers, washer fluid.
- Per schedule: oil and filter, tire rotation, air and cabin filters, brake fluid, coolant, transmission fluid, spark plugs, timing belt (if equipped: failure can destroy the engine).
- Battery: typically lasts ~3-5 years; slow cranking or corrosion are signs; heat shortens life.
- Brakes: squealing (wear indicators), grinding (stop soon), pulsing, pulling, soft pedal (have it checked immediately).

## Warning lights
Red = stop safely soon (oil pressure, coolant temperature, brake system, charging). Flashing check engine light = severe misfire: reduce speed and get it checked promptly to avoid catalytic converter damage. Steady check engine: get the code read (many parts stores do it free; an OBD-II reader works too) and look up the code meaning for the specific model.

## Recalls
Check by VIN at NHTSA (nhtsa.gov/recalls); recall repairs are free at dealers.

## Repairs and quotes
Describe symptoms precisely (when, speed, sound, smell). Get written estimates with parts and labor; compare with fair-price estimators and a second quote for big jobs; ask for old parts back; look for ASE-certified technicians and good reviews. Keep a maintenance log spreadsheet (date, mileage, service, cost, shop) and reminders via schedule.

## Safety
Never get under a car supported only by a jack: use rated jack stands on solid ground. Breakdown: hazard lights, pull far off the road, stay in the car with seatbelt on if on a highway shoulder, call roadside assistance or 911 if unsafe. Winter kit: scraper, blanket, flashlight, jumper cables, water, snacks. Jump-starting: follow the manual's order (positive to positive, negative to a ground point on the dead car).

## Rules
Don't book or pay for service; draft messages to shops for the user to send.
