## Know the site
- USDA Plant Hardiness Zone (planthardiness.ars.usda.gov, by ZIP; outside the US use the national equivalent) for perennials.
- Average last spring and first fall frost dates (local cooperative extension or weather service) for annuals and vegetables.
- Sun hours (full sun = 6+ hours direct), soil type and drainage, a soil test through the local extension service (pH and nutrients) before adding fertilizer or lime.
Save the user's zone, frost dates and beds to memory with their OK.

## Vegetable garden plan
1. Pick 5-8 crops the household eats; easy starts: lettuce, radishes, bush beans, zucchini, tomatoes, herbs.
2. Planting calendar backward from frost dates: cool-season crops (peas, lettuce, spinach, brassicas) before the last frost per seed packets; warm-season (tomatoes, peppers, squash, beans) after it when soil warms. Seed-starting dates indoors are often 6-8 weeks before last frost for tomatoes and peppers.
3. Layout: tall crops on the north side, spacing from seed packets, paths to reach every bed, crop rotation by plant family year to year.
4. Spreadsheet: crop, sow/transplant date, spacing, days to harvest, notes; reminders via schedule.

## Care
Water deeply and less often (about 1 inch per week is a common guideline, more in heat and sandy soil), at the base in the morning; mulch 2-3 inches; feed based on soil test; prune with clean tools at the right season for the plant.

## Problems: diagnose before treating
Ask for photos (image tools can look), plant, symptoms, location, watering, recent weather. Common: yellow lower leaves (overwatering, nitrogen), wilting in heat (water stress or wilt disease), holes (caterpillars, slugs), white powder (powdery mildew), blossom-end rot on tomatoes (uneven watering/calcium uptake). Integrated pest management: identify, tolerate minor damage, hand-pick, barriers, encourage beneficial insects, then least-toxic products. Pesticides: the label is the law; protect pollinators (don't spray open flowers), children and pets; check that plants are pet-safe (ASPCA list).

## Houseplants
Match light (north window low, south bright), water when the top inch is dry for most, drainage holes, avoid overwatering (the most common killer).

## Sources
