## Prepare (most of the value is here)
1. Interests, not positions: what each side really needs (cash flow, certainty, speed, reputation, risk).
2. BATNA: your best alternative if no deal; improve it before negotiating (get competing quotes via web research). Estimate theirs.
3. Reservation price (walk-away point) and target (ambitious but justifiable). ZOPA exists if their walk-away overlaps yours.
4. Issues list: price, term, payment timing, volume, scope, service levels, start date, exclusivity, warranties. Rank by value to you vs cost to them; trade low-cost-to-you items for high-value-to-you ones.
5. Objective criteria: market data, competitor prices, published rates, benchmarks (cite sources).

## At the table
- Anchor first when you have good information; anchor with a reason and a precise number ($48,500 reads as researched).
- When they anchor first, don't counter inside their frame: re-anchor with your criteria.
- Concede slowly, in shrinking steps, and always get something back ("If we sign for 24 months, can you do $X?").
- Ask open questions and use silence; label emotions ("It sounds like timing is the main concern").
- Package offers: 2-3 options of equal value to you, let them choose (MESO).
- Summarize agreements in writing immediately.

## Scripts
- Vendor: "We like your product. We have a quote at $X from Y with similar terms. What can you do on price or term to make this work?"
- Salary/raise: research ranges (BLS, levels data, job posts), list your results with numbers, ask for a specific figure at the top of the justified range, negotiate total package (base, bonus, equity, PTO, title, start date).
- Rent/lease: comparables, longer term for lower rent, free months, improvement allowances, caps on increases, exit clauses.
- Client pushing your price down: trade scope instead of price ("At that budget we can do A and B; C would be a phase 2").

## Ethics and limits
No lying about material facts or fake competing offers. Draft emails for the user; never send or accept offers without the user's approval. Legal terms (liability, IP, non-competes) and big contracts: consult a lawyer before signing.

## Deliverable
One-page prep sheet: interests, BATNA both sides, target/walk-away, issue trade table, opening offer with rationale, likely objections and responses, concession plan.
