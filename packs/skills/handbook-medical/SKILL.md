## Identify the kind of help
Distinguish medical education, a school biology/chemistry question, research appraisal, administrative documentation and a person's actual symptoms. A chemistry mole calculation is school work, not a medical risk signal. Answer routine education at the requested level. For personal health questions, use current authoritative sources and avoid presenting a model response as a diagnosis or treatment decision. Urgent or life-threatening symptoms require immediate local emergency care instead of a prolonged research workflow.

## Choose focused knowledge
Use medical-evidence-lookup for evidence searches, medical-research-appraisal for papers, medical-lab-results for laboratory explanations, medical-medications or drug-information for medication information, and medical-doctor-visit-prep to prepare questions and a history. medical-clinical-documentation organizes supplied facts without inventing observations. medical-coding-billing supports administrative coding with source versions. Read the matching hipaa-* pack before handling identifiable clinical material or a regulated workflow.

## Evidence workflow
Define the clinical/educational question and population. Search authoritative clinical bodies, systematic reviews, government resources and primary literature as appropriate. Open sources rather than relying on search snippets. Note publication date, guideline version, population, exclusions, intervention/comparator, outcomes and follow-up. Distinguish guideline advice from a single study, and correlation from causation. Check corrections, retractions and whether newer evidence changes the conclusion. Track conflicting consequential claims with research_evidence; independent model agreement is not clinical evidence.

## Explain results accurately
Use plain words, then define necessary technical terms. Describe absolute quantities and denominators when available; keep relative effects connected to baseline risk. Explain what a test measures and its stated reference range, without diagnosing from an isolated value. Present uncertainty, applicability and the practical questions to ask a clinician. Do not infer missing exam findings, allergies, medications or pregnancy status. Do not direct a person to start, stop or change a medication or dose; use the current official label and appropriate professional advice.

## Privacy and learning
Ask only for information needed to answer. Avoid copying identities and full records into long-term knowledge notes. Store reusable methods and source references rather than patient-identifiable examples. Keep generated drafts separate from a clinician's signed documentation. An administrative workflow should verify dates, source facts, approvals and the destination before any external submission.

Reference entry: [NLM MedlinePlus: evaluating health information](https://medlineplus.gov/evaluatinghealthinformation.html). Recheck clinical guidance at the time of use; this handbook stores a method, not a clinical database.
