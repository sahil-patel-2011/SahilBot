## Principles
- Price on value to the customer, bounded below by cost and informed by alternatives. Cost-plus sets a floor, not a price.
- Value metric: charge on the unit that grows with the customer's value (seats, usage, transactions, locations). A good value metric makes expansion revenue automatic.
- Packaging matters as much as the number: good/better/best tiers with a clear "most popular" middle tier; put the features that big customers need (SSO, priority support, admin) in the top tier.

## Process
1. Costs: unit cost and fully loaded cost to serve (from QuickBooks/Xero or the user's numbers). Compute gross margin at candidate prices.
2. Alternatives: research 5-10 competitors' public pricing pages with web_search/fetch_page; record price, value metric, tiers, date seen. Include "do nothing / spreadsheet / hire someone" as an alternative.
3. Value: quantify what the customer gains (hours saved x wage, revenue gained, risk avoided). Price often lands at 10-30% of quantified value for B2B; state it as a heuristic.
4. Willingness to pay: Van Westendorp questions (too cheap / bargain / getting expensive / too expensive) for a survey, or Gabor-Granger (would you buy at $X?) for direct price points. In interviews ask about current spend and budgets, not hypotheticals.
5. Choose structure: one-time, subscription, usage, hybrid (platform fee + usage), freemium (only if marginal cost is near zero and there's a natural upgrade trigger).
6. Model it: spreadsheet of tiers x expected mix x churn, with break-even and margin per tier.
7. Test: new-customer-only price tests, A/B on pricing page where traffic allows, or sequential tests by period. Guardrails: conversion, churn, refund rate.

## Raising prices
Grandfather or give 30-60 days' notice to existing customers, explain the added value, offer annual lock-in at the old price. Expect some churn; compute the break-even churn: a 20% rise breaks even if you lose < 1 - 1/1.2 ≈ 16.7% of revenue (ignoring margin effects; with margins included, recompute).

## Discounts
Have a written policy: max %, who approves, trade discounts for something (annual prepay, case study, multi-year). Never discount by default.

## Mini-example
Bookkeeping service at $300/mo, clients save ~10 hrs/mo of owner time at $80/hr = $800 value; competitors $250-$600. Tiers: Essentials $299, Growth $499 (payroll + monthly call), Premium $899 (CFO review). Test Growth at $549 for new clients for 60 days.

## Verify and caveats
Competitor prices need URLs and dates. Price-fixing, resale-price rules and consumer pricing laws vary by country: consult a lawyer before coordinated or regulated pricing. Deliver a short memo plus the pricing model spreadsheet.
