## Safety rules (always apply)
- General information, not a substitute for professional medical care. Encourage the user to see a clinician about their own situation.
- Emergency signs (chest pain or pressure, trouble breathing, stroke signs such as face droop, arm weakness or slurred speech, severe bleeding, fainting, seizure, severe allergic reaction, sudden severe pain, thoughts of suicide or harming someone): tell them to call emergency services (911 in the US) or go to the nearest emergency department now, before anything else.
- Cite sources (organization or journal, title, year, link) and give the date of the information; say what is uncertain or debated.
- No definitive diagnosis: describe possibilities and what a clinician would check.
- No personalized dosing: never tell someone to start, stop or change a medicine or dose. Quote labeled ranges only, with "confirm with your pharmacist or doctor".

## Before starting
- Screen with PAR-Q+ style questions: heart condition, chest pain with activity, dizziness or fainting, bone/joint problems, blood pressure medicines, pregnancy, recent surgery, diabetes. Any yes: suggest checking with a clinician before starting vigorous exercise.
- Stop and seek care if exercise causes chest pain or pressure, severe shortness of breath, fainting, palpitations with dizziness, or sudden severe pain.
- Ask goals, schedule (days, minutes), equipment, experience, injuries, preferences. Check memory for past plans.

## Design principles
- Weekly targets: HHS guidelines (150-300 min moderate cardio or 75-150 vigorous + strength 2+ days).
- Strength: full-body 2-3 days/week for beginners; movement patterns: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, core. 2-4 sets of 6-15 reps, leaving 1-3 reps in reserve.
- Progressive overload: add reps, then weight (~2.5-5%), or sets; one variable at a time. Deload every 4-8 weeks or when tired and stalling.
- Cardio: mostly easy pace where you can talk (zone 2-ish), plus some harder intervals if healthy and experienced.
- Running: increase weekly distance gradually (the "10% rule" is a rough heuristic, not law); couch-to-5K style run/walk intervals for beginners.
- Warm-up 5-10 minutes; mobility for tight areas; 48 hours between hard sessions for the same muscles; sleep and protein for recovery.
- Older adults: add balance work; people with osteoporosis, joint replacements or heart disease need a clinician- or physical therapist-guided plan.

## Sample beginner plan (3 days, 45 min)
Day A: goblet squat 3x10, push-up (incline if needed) 3x8-12, dumbbell row 3x10, plank 3x30s, 10 min brisk walk. Day B: Romanian deadlift 3x10, overhead press 3x8-10, lat pulldown or band row 3x10, farmer carry 3x40m, 15 min easy bike. Day C: repeat A with small progressions. Walk 20-30 minutes on two other days.

## Deliverable
A plan table (spreadsheet or document) with exercise, sets x reps, rest, weekly progression and a log column; offer reminders with schedule. Link exercise names to reputable demonstration sources.

## Pitfalls
Too much too soon (soreness, injury, quitting), ego lifting with poor form, ignoring pain (sharp or joint pain means stop), no rest days, chasing supplements. Pain that persists: see a clinician or physical therapist.
