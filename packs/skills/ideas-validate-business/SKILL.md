## 1. Write the riskiest assumptions
List what must be true: problem exists and hurts, this customer has budget, they will switch from today's solution, you can reach them affordably, you can deliver at a margin. Rank by risk x how cheap to test. Test the top one first.

## 2. Problem discovery interviews (10-20 people)
- Ask about the past, not the future: "Tell me about the last time you dealt with X." "What did you do? What did it cost? What did you try?"
- Avoid pitching and leading questions ("Would you use...?" gives false positives).
- Signals: they already spend money or time on workarounds, they ask when they can have it, they introduce you to others. Compliments are not data.
- Record each interview in a simple sheet: role, pain (1-5), current solution, spend, quote, follow-up.

## 3. Market sizing (show both methods, cite every number)
- Top-down: industry figure from a credible source (government data such as Census/BLS, industry associations, public filings), narrowed by segment.
- Bottom-up (preferred): number of target customers x annual price. Example: 18,000 independent dental practices in region (source) x $2,400/yr = $43M SAM.
- TAM = everyone with the problem; SAM = those your model and geography can serve; SOM = realistic share in 3-5 years given channels (often low single-digit % of SAM). Use web_search/fetch_page or agents in parallel; record URL and date.

## 4. Competitor research
Table: name, target customer, price, positioning, strengths, weaknesses (from reviews), funding/size. Include indirect alternatives (spreadsheets, agencies, doing nothing). Crunchbase or Similarweb connectors if available.

## 5. Smoke tests and MVPs (cheapest first)
Landing page with a clear offer and price plus "join waitlist/pre-order" (track visit to signup %); concierge MVP (deliver manually); Wizard-of-Oz (looks automated, manual behind); pre-sales or paid pilots (strongest evidence); a single-feature prototype. Never take real payments for something you can't deliver; refund promptly if you cancel.

## 6. Kill, pivot or persevere criteria (set BEFORE testing)
Example: "Proceed if, within 4 weeks, at least 5 of 15 interviewees describe the pain as top-3 and 3 agree to a paid pilot at $500/month; landing page converts > 5% of targeted visitors. Otherwise pivot the segment or kill."

## Deliverable
Validation report (docx/pdf): assumptions, evidence, market sizing math with citations, competitor table, test results vs. criteria, recommendation. Spreadsheet for sizing and interview log.

## Pitfalls
Confirmation bias; counting friends; sizing with an unsourced "1% of a huge market"; building for months before talking to customers. For regulated products (health, finance, food, kids) consult a lawyer early.
