## 1. Write the riskiest assumptions
Check the brain first (memory search, `vault` search) for earlier research on this idea. Then list what must be true: the problem exists and hurts, this customer has budget, they will switch from today's solution, you can reach them affordably, you can deliver at a margin. Rank by risk x cheapness to test; test the top one first.

## 2. Evidence the pain is real
Run business-complaint-mining for the problem: short quoted complaints with links and dates, pain clusters, frequency, intensity and willingness-to-pay signals. If you can't find people complaining or paying for workarounds, say so plainly: that is the most important finding.

## 3. Competition
Run ideas-novelty-check: who already does this, at what price, for whom, and what their reviews say they miss. Competitor table: name, link, target customer, price, positioning, strengths, weaknesses (from reviews), funding/size, last sign of life. Include indirect alternatives (spreadsheets, agencies, doing nothing).

## 4. Market sizing (both methods, cite every number)
- Bottom-up (preferred): target customers x annual price. Example: 18,000 independent dental practices (source) x $2,400/yr = $43M SAM.
- Top-down: a credible industry figure (Census/BLS, industry associations, public filings) narrowed by segment.
- TAM = everyone with the problem; SAM = those your model and geography can serve; SOM = realistic 3-5 year share given channels (often low single-digit % of SAM). Record URL and date for each input; label assumptions.

## 5. Scorecard and verdict
Score 1-5 with the evidence behind each: pain severity, frequency, willingness to pay, competition (5 = open gap), feasibility for this founder (skills, budget, time, licenses), market size. Verdict: Go / Reshape (which segment, price or form) / Kill, with the one fact that would change it.

## 6. Cheapest next test (set kill criteria BEFORE running it)
- Landing-page smoke test: headline in customers' words, price shown, one CTA, traffic source and $ budget, success threshold.
- 5-15 problem interviews (Mom Test: ask about the last time it happened, what they did, what it cost; never "would you use...?"). Log role, pain 1-5, current solution, spend, quote, follow-up. Compliments are not data; money, time and intros are.
- Concierge or Wizard-of-Oz MVP: deliver by hand behind a simple front.
- Pre-sale or paid pilot: the strongest evidence. Never take money for something you can't deliver; refund promptly if you cancel.
Example criteria: "Proceed if within 4 weeks at least 5 of 15 interviewees rank the pain top-3 and 3 agree to a paid pilot at $500/month; the landing page converts > 5% of targeted visitors. Otherwise reshape the segment or kill."

## Deliverable
Validation report (docx/pdf): assumptions, complaint evidence, competitor table, sizing math with citations, scorecard, verdict, test plan with criteria, results when available. Spreadsheet for sizing and the interview log. Save the verdict to the vault.

## Pitfalls
Confirmation bias; counting friends and family; unsourced "1% of a huge market"; building for months before talking to customers; ignoring that nobody pays for the workaround today. For regulated products (health, finance, food, kids) consult a lawyer early.
