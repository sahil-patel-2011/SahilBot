## Recipes
- Ask: ingredients on hand, servings, time, equipment, skill level, diet and allergies (memory first).
- Use reputable recipe sources (tested-recipe sites, cookbooks the user owns in the knowledge vault) and cite the link; or write a clear original recipe.
- Format: servings, active and total time, ingredients with amounts in the user's units (offer metric/US), numbered steps with times and visual cues ("until golden and the edges bubble"), make-ahead and storage notes.
- Scaling: multiply ingredients; don't scale salt, spices, leavening and cooking times linearly for large changes; pan size changes affect baking time.

## Substitutions (common)
Buttermilk: 1 cup milk + 1 tbsp lemon juice or vinegar, rest 5 minutes. Egg (baking, binding): 1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water. Self-rising flour: 1 cup all-purpose + 1 1/2 tsp baking powder + 1/4 tsp salt. Fresh to dried herbs: about 3:1. Wine in sauces: stock plus a splash of vinegar.

## Food safety (USDA)
- Safe minimum internal temperatures: poultry 165°F (74°C); ground meats 160°F (71°C) (ground poultry 165°F); beef, pork, veal, lamb steaks/roasts/chops 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest; fish 145°F; leftovers and casseroles reheated to 165°F. Use a food thermometer.
- 2-hour rule: perishables out no more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F/32°C). Fridge 40°F (4°C) or below; leftovers 3-4 days.
- Don't wash raw chicken (spreads bacteria); separate cutting boards for raw meat; thaw in fridge, cold water or microwave.
- Allergies: read labels for the major allergens (US: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame); avoid cross-contact.
- When in doubt, throw it out. Signs of food poisoning with dehydration, bloody stools, high fever or neurological symptoms need medical care.

## Kitchen safety
Grease fire: turn off heat, cover with a lid or use baking soda; never water. Keep handles turned in; knives sharp.

## Fixes
Too salty: add more unsalted base (more vegetables, stock, grains) plus a little acid or fat; the "add a potato" trick barely helps. Too spicy: dairy, sugar, acid, more base. Broken sauce: whisk in a spoon of warm water or a fresh yolk (emulsions). Dry meat: slice thin and sauce it.
