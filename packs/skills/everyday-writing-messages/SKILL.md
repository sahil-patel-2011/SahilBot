## First
Who is it to, relationship, goal (what should happen after they read it), channel (text, email, letter, note), the facts, any deadline, and the user's usual voice (check memory and past messages they share). Ask once if the goal is unclear.

## Tone recipes
- Polite request: context in one line, the specific ask, why it matters (briefly), an easy next step, thanks.
- Firm but civil: state the issue factually, the impact, what you need and by when, the consequence if not resolved (only ones the user is willing to carry out), no insults or sarcasm.
- Apology: acknowledge exactly what happened, take responsibility without excuses, name the impact on them, what you'll do to make it right, how you'll prevent it. Avoid "sorry if you were offended".
- Complaint to a company: order or account number, dates, what happened, what you've tried, the resolution you want (refund, replacement, credit), a reasonable deadline; attach evidence; keep it short. Escalation path: supervisor → written complaint → regulator or consumer protection agency (e.g. state attorney general, CFPB for financial products), card chargeback per the issuer's rules.
- Saying no: thanks, clear no, brief reason optional, alternative if genuine.
- Condolence: simple, sincere, a specific memory if they have one, an offer of concrete help; no "everything happens for a reason".
- Thank-you: specific about what they did and its effect.

## Craft
Lead with the point; one message, one purpose; short paragraphs; plain words; remove hedges and over-apologizing in firm messages; match formality to the relationship. Offer 2 versions when tone is a judgment call (warmer / more direct). Proofread names, dates, amounts.

## Sending rule
Draft only. Never send emails, texts or posts without the user's explicit OK; for email use the mail tool's drafts (email-drafting skill) so they can review.

## Pitfalls
Writing angry (suggest waiting an hour and sending the calmer version), threats or legal claims the user can't back up (for legal disputes, suggest consulting a lawyer), sharing more personal information than needed.
