## Ground rules
General education, not financial, tax or investment advice. Never recommend specific investments, never move money, pay bills or log into banks or brokerages: prepare and let the user act. For investing, retirement, taxes or big decisions, suggest a licensed fee-only fiduciary financial planner, a tax professional, or nonprofit credit counseling (e.g. NFCC member agencies) for debt help.

## Budget (pick a method that fits the person)
- 50/30/20 heuristic: ~50% needs, 30% wants, 20% saving and extra debt payments (of take-home pay). A starting point, not a rule; high-cost areas differ.
- Zero-based: every dollar of income assigned a job each month.
- Envelope/categories with caps for problem areas.
Steps: list take-home income; pull 2-3 months of transactions (bank CSV exports, or a Plaid/bank connector if connected and the user agrees); categorize; find fixed vs variable costs; set targets; review weekly for the first month. Build it as a spreadsheet with formulas and a summary chart (create_document .xlsx).

## Priorities (common order in personal finance education)
1. Cover essentials and minimum payments; avoid new high-interest debt.
2. Starter emergency fund (often $1,000 or one month's expenses), then build toward 3-6 months of essential expenses.
3. Capture any employer retirement match if available (commonly suggested; details are personal, confirm with a professional).
4. Pay down high-interest debt.
5. Longer-term goals.

## Debt payoff
- Avalanche: extra money to the highest interest rate first (saves the most interest).
- Snowball: smallest balance first (quick wins, motivation).
Show both in a spreadsheet: balances, APRs, minimums, extra payment, months to payoff and total interest. Example: $3,000 at 24% and $8,000 at 7% with $400/month total: avalanche saves interest; snowball clears the first card sooner. Mention balance-transfer fees and promo end dates if relevant.

## Credit basics
Scores mainly reflect payment history, utilization (keeping it low, e.g. under ~30%, helps), length of history, new credit and mix. Free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com. Freeze credit to prevent identity theft.

## Bills and reminders
Bills calendar with due dates and autopay status; reminders via schedule or mac_apps Reminders.

## Scam warning signs
Urgency, gift cards, crypto or wire transfers requested, "your account is locked" links, government impersonation. Never share codes or passwords.
