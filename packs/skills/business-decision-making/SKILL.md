## 1. Frame
What exactly is being decided, by when, by whom, and what does success look like in 1 year? Is it reversible (two-way door: decide fast, learn) or irreversible (one-way door: slow down, gather evidence)? Avoid false binaries: generate at least 3 options including "wait/test first" and "do a small version".

## 2. Criteria and weights
List 4-7 criteria (cost, upside, risk, time, fit with goals, reversibility, effect on people). Weight them (sum to 100) before scoring options, so preferences don't bend the weights. Score each option 1-5; show the weighted table in a spreadsheet; then sanity-check against gut feel and discuss disagreements (they often reveal a missing criterion).

## 3. Quantify uncertainty
- Expected value: sum of probability x net payoff. Example: a launch costing $50k has a 30% chance of netting +$250k, 50% of -$10k, 20% of -$50k (total loss): EV = 75 - 5 - 10 = +$60k. Show the arithmetic and label every probability as an estimate.
- Use ranges (low/base/high) instead of single numbers; ask "what would have to be true?"
- Base rates: start from how often similar things succeed (e.g. survival rates of new businesses from BLS data) before adjusting for specifics. Cite.
- Value of information: if a cheap test could change the decision, run it first.
- Consider downside you can't survive (ruin): avoid options with a meaningful chance of catastrophic loss even if EV is positive.

## 4. Stress test
Pre-mortem: "It's a year later and this failed. Why?" List causes, then mitigations or early warning signs. Also steelman the option you're leaning against. Check biases: sunk cost, confirmation, overconfidence, anchoring, status quo.

## 5. Decide and record
Decision log entry: date, decision, options considered, key assumptions, expected outcome, review date. Save to memory or the knowledge vault and set a review reminder with schedule. Judge decisions by process and information at the time, not just outcome.

## Output
A short memo: recommendation, why, the matrix, EV/ranges, key risks with mitigations, what would change the decision, next step.

## Caveats
For legal, tax, medical or investment decisions, lay out the trade-offs but recommend consulting the right professional (lawyer, accountant, doctor, licensed financial advisor); never give personalized investment advice.
