## Method
1. Pick one small area (a drawer, a shelf) for a quick win, then expand: category by category (clothes, books, papers, kitchen, sentimental last) or room by room.
2. Five bins: keep, donate, sell, recycle/e-waste, trash. Optional "maybe" box dated; donate it if unopened in 3-6 months.
3. Decision questions: Have I used it in the last year? Would I buy it again today? Do I have a duplicate? Does it fit my space and life now? Is it worth the space it takes?
4. Sentimental items: keep the best few, photograph the rest; a memory box with a size limit.
5. Remove outgoing items the same day or week (car trunk → donation center).

## Organize what stays
Everything gets a home near where it's used; clear bins and labels; vertical storage; heaviest low, rare-use high; drawer dividers; one-in-one-out rule for clothes, toys, mugs.

## Sell, donate, dispose
Sell valuable items (marketplaces; take good photos, research sold prices, meet safely in public places). Donate to local charities (check what they accept). E-waste and batteries to proper recycling (search the city's site; wipe devices first). Hazardous items (paint, chemicals) to household hazardous waste days. Keep donation receipts if itemizing taxes.

## Plan and habits
Session plan (20-60 minutes, timer), a room-by-room checklist spreadsheet with progress, reminders for weekly sessions (schedule). Daily 10-minute reset, monthly donation bag.

## Pitfalls
Buying organizers before decluttering; doing the whole house in one weekend and burning out; deciding for other household members (ask them); keeping items for "someday" indefinitely.

## Notes
Hoarding disorder is a mental health condition: if clutter threatens safety or causes great distress, be gentle and suggest professional help (see health-mental-health-support).
