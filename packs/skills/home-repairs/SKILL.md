## Safety first (always state the relevant ones)
- Gas smell or hissing: leave immediately, don't flip switches, call the gas company or 911 from outside.
- Electrical: turn off the breaker, then verify dead with a non-contact voltage tester; never work on live circuits; water + electricity = stop. Burning smell, scorch marks, warm outlets or a breaker that trips again right after reset: call a licensed electrician.
- Water: know the main shutoff and fixture shutoffs; shut water off first.
- Older homes: paint before 1978 may contain lead (EPA RRP rules for disturbing it); some older materials contain asbestos: don't sand, cut or demolish suspicious materials; test or hire a pro.
- Ladders, power tools, eye and ear protection, gloves; read the tool's manual.
- Carbon monoxide alarm sounding: get outside and call emergency services.

## Call a licensed pro when
Gas lines or appliances, electrical panels and new circuits, structural changes (load-bearing walls, foundations), roofs, main sewer lines, major water damage or mold over about 10 square feet (EPA guidance), anything requiring a permit, or when the user is unsure. Suggest getting 3 quotes, checking license and insurance, reviews and a written estimate.

## Diagnose
Ask: what happens, since when, make/model (find the manual online), photos if possible (image tools can look at them), what they've tried. Work from simplest cause to most complex.

## Common fixes (outline steps; link a reputable how-to or the manufacturer's guide)
- Running toilet: check flapper seal and chain length, fill valve height, overflow tube; replacing a flapper is cheap and common.
- Dripping faucet: identify type (cartridge, ball, compression, ceramic disc); shut off water, replace cartridge or washer with a matching part (bring the old one to the store).
- Slow drain: remove and clean the stopper/hair; plunger; drain snake. Avoid mixing chemical drain cleaners or using them before snaking (splash burns).
- Small drywall hole: mesh patch or backing, joint compound in thin coats, sand, prime, paint.
- Tripped breaker: unplug devices on the circuit, reset fully off then on; repeated trips: overload or fault, call an electrician. GFCI outlets: press reset; find the upstream GFCI.

## Deliverable
Step list with tools and parts (with estimated cost), time estimate, difficulty, safety notes, and the "stop and call a pro if" line. Shopping for parts: everyday-shopping-research (never buy without the user's click).

## Pitfalls
Wrong replacement part, overtightening plumbing fittings, painting over moisture problems, skipping permits for work that requires them.
