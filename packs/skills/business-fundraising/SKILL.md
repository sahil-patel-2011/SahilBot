## First: should they raise at all?
Options: revenue/bootstrapping, customer prepayments, grants (SBIR/STTR in the US for R&D), loans (SBA, bank, revenue-based financing), friends and family, angels, venture capital. VC suits businesses that can plausibly become very large; many good businesses shouldn't take VC.

## What investors look for
Team (why you, domain insight), market (large and growing; bottom-up sizing), traction (revenue, growth rate, retention, engagement, pilots), product insight ("secret"), business model and unit economics, why now, and a clear use of funds tied to milestones that unlock the next round.

## Deck structure (10-12 slides; see pitch-deck skill for building the file)
Title/one-liner; problem; solution and demo; why now; market size; traction; business model; competition (positioning, not a feature checklist); go-to-market; team; financials and milestones; the ask (amount, instrument, use of funds, runway it buys). One message per slide, headline states it, numbers sourced. Build with create_document (.pptx), talk track in speaker notes.

## Instruments in plain terms
- SAFE: investor gives money now for shares later, usually at the next priced round, often with a valuation cap and/or discount. Simple, but stacked SAFEs can cause surprise dilution.
- Convertible note: similar but is debt with interest and a maturity date.
- Priced round: shares sold at a set price per share; pre-money valuation + investment = post-money. New investor % = investment / post-money.
- Option pool: shares reserved for employees; often created before the round, diluting founders.

## Dilution example (illustrative)
Founders own 10M shares (100%). Raise $2M at $8M pre-money: post = $10M, investors get 20%. If a 10% post-money pool is also created pre-money, founders end near 70%. Build a simple cap table spreadsheet: holder, shares, % fully diluted, after each round; use formulas so the user can change terms.

## Running the raise
Target list (stage, sector, check size; Crunchbase connector if available), warm intros, a 4-8 week tight process, CRM sheet tracking stage and next step (HubSpot/Pipedrive if connected), data room (financial model, cap table, contracts), follow-ups. Never send messages to investors without the user's approval.

## Rules and caveats
- This is general education, not investment advice or a recommendation to invest or sell securities.
- Securities laws govern who you can raise from and how you advertise (e.g. accredited investor rules in the US). Always consult a startup lawyer before signing a SAFE, term sheet or issuing shares, and an accountant for tax effects (e.g. 83(b) elections, QSBS).
- Don't inflate traction; investors check.
